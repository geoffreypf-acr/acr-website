Vehicle security for the Ferrari

Almost every Ferrari we protect is stored rather than parked. It lives in a garage, a car barn or a specialist facility, it covers low mileage, and it spends most of the year stationary on a conditioner. That single fact changes the security conversation more than the badge does: the threats to a stored car are theft by transporter and theft from within a facility, and the constraint on any hard-wired device is a battery that is already doing very little.

What we recommend for a Ferrari, and why

  • Independently powered tracking with motion alerting — catches a car being loaded, which is how a Ferrari is actually taken.
  • Meta Trak S5 with ADR driver tags — for shared or managed storage, where legitimate movement needs distinguishing from theft.
  • Battery and conditioner review — before anything is hard-wired into a car that sits for months.
  • Autowatch Ghost II — on the modern cars, where the CAN bus supports it and the car is driven regularly.

How a Ferrari actually gets stolen

A Ferrari is rarely driven away. It is loaded, and the thief does not need the key to do it. Which means an ignition-triggered alert is useless and a well-placed tracker with independent power and motion sensing is the thing that matters - it should raise an alarm when the car tilts onto a ramp, not when someone turns a key that nobody is going to turn. Where a Ferrari is kept in shared or managed storage, the second consideration is who has legitimate access, which is what driver-recognition tags are for.

What fitting a Ferrari involves

Two things govern a Ferrari fitting. The first is the battery: a hard-wired tracker draws a small standing current, and on a car doing five hundred miles a year that current is not trivial. We check battery and conditioner setup first and give you an honest answer about whether the installation is safe for how the car is actually used. The second is the interior. Trim and carbon are not to be levered at, and on a mid-engined car the routing options are genuinely limited. We work slowly, use existing looms, and everything is reversible.

Questions

Frequently asked

Will a tracker flatten my Ferrari's battery?
It is the right question to ask, and it is the first thing we check. A hard-wired tracker draws a small standing current, which matters on a car covering a few hundred miles a year. We look at the battery and your conditioner setup and give you a straight answer before we fit - sometimes the honest advice is to sort the charging first.
The car is loaded onto a transporter, not driven. Does a tracker help?
Only the right kind. An alert tied to the ignition will never fire, because nobody turns a key. What you need is independent power and motion sensing, so the alarm is raised when the car tilts onto a ramp. That is what we fit on stored cars.
The car is in shared storage. Anything different?
Yes - driver-recognition tags. In a managed facility cars get moved legitimately, and a plain tracker cannot tell an authorised shuffle from a theft. With ADR tags your own movements are silent and anyone else's are not.
Do you come to me, or do I bring the car to you?
We come to you. ACR Automobile is a fully mobile service — we fit at your home, office or storage across London, Surrey and the Home Counties, and there is no workshop to visit.
Will it affect my Ferrari warranty?
No. We install to manufacturer standard, taking factory feeds using manufacturer connector standards, and nothing is cut or spliced. Your warranty is unaffected and the installation is reversible.
